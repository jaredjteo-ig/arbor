## Executive Summary

Arbor is not an HRIS with AI features. It is an AI HR department governed by PACT so that a 10-person company can deploy agent-filled roles that handle payroll, leave, compliance, and onboarding — all within defined operating envelopes, with the boss as human-on-the-loop. The D/T/R structure is not governance overhead; it is the company's agent workforce plan. This repositioning transforms Arbor from a commodity software product competing with dozens of HRIS vendors into a category-defining platform that fills roles companies cannot afford to hire for.

## 1. The Problem

### 1.1 Singapore SME Workforce Reality

Singapore has approximately 280,000 SMEs. Roughly 70% — about 196,000 companies — have fewer than 10 employees. These are hawker stall chains, logistics firms, F&B outlets, architectural practices, accounting firms, and small retail operations.

These companies share a structural problem: **HR is a department they cannot afford.**

| HR Function             | Market Rate (Monthly) | What Happens Without It                          |
| ----------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| HR Manager              | $5,000-$8,000         | Boss does everything; balls get dropped          |
| Payroll Officer         | $3,000-$5,000         | Boss uses Excel; CPF calculations go wrong       |
| Compliance Officer      | $4,000-$6,000         | Nobody monitors regulatory changes; fines happen |
| Recruitment Coordinator | $2,500-$4,000         | Boss posts on JobsDB; no structured pipeline     |
| HR Admin                | $2,000-$3,500         | Paper forms, lost documents, no audit trail      |

A fully staffed HR function costs $16,500-$26,500 per month. For a company with 8 employees generating $40,000/month in revenue, dedicating 40-66% of revenue to HR is absurd. So they don't. They cope.

### 1.2 How SMEs Cope Today

**Pattern 1: The Boss Does Everything**

The most common pattern. The boss handles payroll in Excel, approves leave via WhatsApp, files CPF submissions manually, and handles MOM compliance by guessing. This works until it doesn't — a wrong CPF calculation, a missed IR8A filing, a PDPA violation they didn't know about.

**Pattern 2: The Ah Mei Pattern**

One person — often the admin assistant or office manager — does HR, finance, admin, and reception. Ah Mei is the company's entire back office. She is competent but overloaded. She has no backup, no training budget, and no time to stay current on regulatory changes. When Ah Mei leaves, the company's institutional knowledge walks out the door.

**Pattern 3: Outsource to Consultants**

The boss pays an HR consultant $200-500 per engagement for specific tasks: annual IR8A filing, employment contract drafting, MOM work pass applications. This is expensive per-task but cheaper than a full-time hire. The problem: consultants are reactive, not proactive. Nobody is watching the store between engagements. The company discovers compliance gaps only when something goes wrong.

**Pattern 4: Buy an HRIS and Hope for the Best**

The boss subscribes to a payroll/leave system (HReasily, Talenox, JustLogin). These tools handle the mechanics — payslip generation, leave tracking, attendance recording. But they don't make decisions. The boss still decides who gets approved for leave, whether the CPF submission looks right, whether the new employee's contract complies with EA requirements. The tool is a record-keeper, not an HR department.

### 1.3 The Compliance Risk

Every coping pattern above carries compliance risk. Singapore employment law is not optional:

| Regulation                                       | Penalty for Non-Compliance                                 | Common SME Violation                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Employment Act (EA)                              | Fines up to $5,000 per charge                              | Incorrect overtime calculation, wrong notice periods             |
| CPF Act                                          | Surcharge of 18% per annum on late/incorrect contributions | Wrong contribution rates, missed monthly filings                 |
| PDPA                                             | Fines up to $1,000,000                                     | Employee NRIC displayed publicly, no data protection policy      |
| Employment of Foreign Manpower Act (EFMA)        | Fines up to $30,000, imprisonment up to 12 months          | Incorrect FWL, wrong pass category, expired permits              |
| Workplace Safety and Health Act (WSH)            | Fines up to $200,000, imprisonment up to 2 years           | Missing safety records for field workers                         |
| Workforce Australia/Fair Consideration Framework | MOM scrutiny, EP rejection                                 | Not advertising jobs on MyCareersFuture before hiring foreigners |

A 10-person company with no dedicated HR is one mistake away from a $5,000 fine that represents a week's revenue. The boss doesn't know what they don't know.

## 2. The Solution: Agent-Filled HR Roles

### 2.1 The Repositioning

Arbor does not add AI features to an HRIS. Arbor fills HR roles with PACT-governed AI agents.

The D/T/R tree that PACT provides is not a governance diagram. It is the company's **agent workforce plan**. Some roles are filled by humans. Some are filled by agents. Some are filled by humans augmented by shadow agents. PACT ensures that agent-filled roles operate within defined operating envelopes — they can do exactly what the role requires, and nothing more.

**The mental model for the boss**:

"I hired an AI HR Manager for $200/month. It handles leave approvals, processes payroll, tracks compliance deadlines, and onboards new employees. It cannot approve terminations, access medical records, or exceed budget limits. If something unusual happens, it asks me. I set the rules once; the system enforces them continuously."

This is not a chatbot. This is not an assistant. This is a governed agent filling a role that the company cannot afford to fill with a human.

### 2.2 How It Works

When a boss registers on Arbor and adds employees:

1. **PACT auto-generates the organizational tree** from employee data (departments, designations, reporting lines). This happens invisibly — no configuration screens.

2. **Template envelopes are assigned** to every role based on job titles. The HR Manager agent gets an envelope that allows leave management, attendance tracking, employee onboarding, and payroll viewing. It cannot approve payroll runs (that's held for the boss), cannot submit CPF (held for the boss), and cannot access medical records (blocked).

3. **Agent-fillable roles are identified**. In a 10-person company, the HR Manager, Payroll Officer, Leave Administrator, Attendance Tracker, Claims Processor, and Compliance Monitor are all agent-fillable. The boss and the employees are human. Ah Mei, if she exists, is human with shadow augmentation.

4. **Agents are activated progressively**. Week 1: leave administrator handles balance tracking. Week 2: leave approvals within policy. Month 1: payroll calculation and payslip generation. Month 2: compliance monitoring and filing reminders. By month 6, the company has a full AI HR department.

5. **The boss is human-on-the-loop**. Routine actions (leave within policy, payslip generation, attendance recording) happen automatically. Unusual actions (leave during notice period, payroll variance above 10%) are flagged. Significant actions (statutory filing, termination processing) are held for boss approval. Dangerous actions (deleting audit records, overriding statutory rates) are blocked.

### 2.3 The PACT Difference

Without PACT, an "AI HR assistant" is a chatbot with API access. It can do anything the boss can do, with no structural limits on its authority. The boss must trust the AI to do the right thing — and if it doesn't, the boss is liable.

With PACT:

- **Operating envelopes** define exactly what each agent role can and cannot do. The Payroll Agent can calculate CPF but cannot modify CPF rates. The Leave Agent can approve leave within policy but cannot override policy limits. These are structural constraints, not hope.

- **The verification gradient** calibrates human oversight. Not every action needs the boss's attention. The gradient determines which actions flow through automatically, which generate a notification, which pause for approval, and which are hard-blocked. This is configurable by observation — the shadow agent watches what happens and suggests adjustments.

- **Knowledge clearance** controls data access. The Leave Agent sees leave balances and attendance records (RESTRICTED). The Payroll Agent sees salary data and CPF records (CONFIDENTIAL). Neither sees medical certificates (SECRET). This is structural data classification, enforced at every access point.

- **EATP trust lineage** records every action, every decision, every governance change. When MOM asks "who approved this termination?", the answer is a cryptographic audit trail showing the human boss confirmed the action at 2:47 PM on Tuesday, within the governance framework established on the company's registration date.

## 3. Why Not Just "AI HRIS"?

### 3.1 The Commodity Trap

Every HRIS vendor is adding AI features. HReasily is adding "AI payroll assistant." Talenox is adding "smart leave recommendations." Deel and Rippling have AI onboarding. Within 18 months, every HRIS on the market will have an AI chatbot that can answer questions and generate reports.

If Arbor positions as "HRIS with AI features," it competes on feature parity with funded companies. HReasily (Series B, $60M) and Deel ($12B valuation) will always have more features, more integrations, more marketing budget. Arbor loses this fight.

### 3.2 What Cannot Be Commoditized

PACT governance cannot be copied by adding a feature. It is an architectural commitment that requires:

- A formal organizational grammar (D/T/R addressing)
- Five-dimensional operating envelopes (Financial, Operational, Temporal, Data Access, Communication)
- A verification gradient with calibrated human oversight
- Knowledge clearance independent of organizational rank
- Tamper-evident trust lineage on every action (EATP)

Bolting these onto an existing HRIS is the equivalent of bolting crash safety onto a car designed without it. It can be done poorly (adding a permission check here, an approval workflow there), but it cannot be done structurally without rebuilding the architecture.

Arbor is built PACT-native. The organizational structure IS the application architecture. This is not a feature competitors can copy in a sprint cycle.

### 3.3 The Trust Moat

The real differentiator is trust. When an SME boss deploys an AI HR department, the question is not "can it do the work?" — any LLM with API access can process a payroll calculation. The question is "can I trust it not to mess up?"

PACT answers this question structurally:

- **"What if the agent approves leave it shouldn't?"** — The operating envelope defines exactly which leave types and durations the agent can auto-approve. Outside the envelope, the action is held for the boss.

- **"What if the agent calculates CPF wrong?"** — Payroll is deterministic (zero LLM, pure arithmetic). The agent runs the calculation within the Payroll Agent envelope. Statutory filing is held for boss confirmation before submission.

- **"What if the agent accesses data it shouldn't?"** — Knowledge clearance prevents the Leave Agent from seeing salary data, the Payroll Agent from seeing medical records, and employees from seeing each other's records. This is structural, not policy.

- **"What if I want to see what the agent did?"** — EATP trust lineage records every action with a tamper-evident audit trail. Every payslip generated, every leave approved, every compliance check performed has a verifiable record.

No amount of "AI features" provides this answer. Only governance does.

## 6. Value Propositions

These are the value propositions as the boss would understand them. No jargon. No technology. Business outcomes.

### 6.1 "Never miss a CPF filing"

The Payroll Agent handles CPF calculations using tested, deterministic arithmetic. It knows the 2026 rates, the OW ceiling ($8,000/month), the AW ceiling, the age-band contribution tables. It generates the CPF e-Submit file and holds it for the boss to confirm and submit. The boss gets a reminder 5 days before the filing deadline, with the file already prepared.

Current state: The boss does CPF calculations in Excel, prays the rates are correct, and sometimes files late because they forgot the deadline.

### 6.2 "Leave approvals in seconds, not days"

The Leave Agent checks the employee's balance, checks whether the requested dates overlap with other team members on leave, checks whether the employee is on notice period or probation, and approves if everything is within policy. The boss gets a notification but doesn't need to act. If the leave is unusual (entire team requesting the same week, leave during critical project period), the agent flags it and asks the boss.

Current state: Employee sends a WhatsApp message to the boss. Boss sees it 6 hours later. Approves without checking the calendar. Discovers two people are on leave the same week when it's too late.

### 6.3 "Compliant by default"

The Compliance Agent monitors Singapore employment law changes. When CPF rates change (January and September), it updates the system automatically. When MOM issues new guidelines (Fair Consideration Framework updates, TAFEP advisories), it flags the relevant impacts to the boss. It tracks filing deadlines (CPF monthly, IR8A annual, NS makeup pay, public holiday pay) and ensures nothing is missed.

Current state: The boss doesn't know about the CPF rate change until the accountant asks why the numbers don't match. The boss doesn't know about the new paternity leave rules (28 days as of 2025) until an employee tells them.

### 6.4 "Your HR Manager costs $200/month"

A full-time HR Manager costs $5,000-$8,000/month plus CPF. Arbor's AI HR department — Payroll Agent + Leave Agent + Compliance Agent + Attendance Agent + Claims Agent + Advisory Agent — costs $200/month flat. That is 2.5-4% of the human cost.

The AI HR department is available 24/7, never takes leave, never resigns, never takes institutional knowledge with them, and maintains a perfect audit trail of every action.

Current state: The boss pays $5,000/month for a human HR manager, or pays nothing and does it themselves (badly), or pays $500-2,000/month for a consultant who works 2 days a month.

### 6.5 "Know what your agents are doing"

Every action by every agent is recorded with a tamper-evident audit trail. The boss can see: what the Payroll Agent calculated, what the Leave Agent approved, what the Compliance Agent flagged. The morning briefing summarizes yesterday's agent activity in plain language. No surprises.

Current state: The boss has no visibility into what their HR manager is doing day-to-day. They find out about problems after they become crises.

### 6.6 "Grow without growing pains"

When the company grows from 10 to 25 to 50 employees, the agent roles scale automatically. The shadow agent observes changing patterns and suggests structural adaptations: "Your Operations team has grown to 12 people. Want me to set up a team lead with their own approval authority?" This is organic, governed restructuring — not a painful HR transformation project.

Current state: Growth creates chaos. The boss who managed 10 people directly now manages 30 and drops balls constantly. Someone eventually forces a reorganization that takes 3 months and breaks everything.

## 7. Unique Selling Points

These are structural differentiators that competitors cannot copy by adding a feature.

### 7.1 PACT Governance (Open Specification)

PACT is a published specification under CC BY 4.0, maintained by the Terrene Foundation. It provides the formal organizational grammar (D/T/R), five-dimensional operating envelopes, verification gradient, knowledge clearance, and bridge definitions. Because it is an open specification, any platform can implement it — but Arbor is the reference implementation for HRIS, designed PACT-native from the ground up.

A competitor adding "governance features" would need to adopt or reinvent the entire PACT framework. Adding a permission check or an approval workflow is not the same as structural governance.

### 7.2 Agent-Filled Roles with Verifiable Envelopes

Arbor does not add "AI assistance" to existing workflows. It defines HR roles (HR Manager, Payroll Officer, Compliance Monitor) as formal positions in the organizational tree, with operating envelopes that constrain what the agent filling that role can do. The envelope is inspectable, auditable, and verifiable.

When the boss says "What can my Payroll Agent do?", the answer is a concrete list of capabilities and constraints — not "it uses GPT-4 so it can probably handle it."

### 7.3 Trust Lineage on Every HR Action (EATP Records)

Every payslip generated, every leave approved, every compliance check performed creates a tamper-evident EATP record. This is not application logging — it is cryptographic trust lineage that can be independently verified.

When an employee disputes a payroll calculation or MOM audits a company's CPF submissions, the trust chain shows exactly what happened, who approved it, within what governance framework, at what time. This is compliance evidence that no traditional HRIS provides.

### 7.4 Progressive Agent Deployment

Arbor does not ask the boss to "set up AI." It progressively activates agent capabilities as the boss builds trust. Week 1: data entry and record-keeping. Week 2: leave balance tracking. Month 1: leave approvals within policy. Month 2: payroll preparation. Each activation requires only the boss's confirmation ("Want me to handle this?"). The system earns trust through demonstrated competence within narrow envelopes before asking for broader authority.

This mirrors CARE's Evolutionary Trust principle: trust is earned through demonstrated competence, not granted upfront.

### 7.5 Open Source Under Terrene Foundation

Arbor is open source under Apache 2.0, maintained by the Terrene Foundation (Singapore CLG). It is fully independent of any commercial entity. This means:

- No vendor lock-in. The boss's data and governance structure are portable.
- Community-driven development. Singapore HR professionals, accountants, and employment lawyers can contribute regulatory knowledge.
- Transparent governance. The code that manages employee data is inspectable.
- Foundation independence. No commercial entity can buy, close-source, or monetize Arbor exclusively.

## 10. Decision Points

These questions require stakeholder input before proceeding to implementation:

1. **Pricing model**: Flat $200/month, or per-employee pricing ($10-20/employee/month)? Flat pricing is simpler to communicate but may undercharge medium SMEs (26-50 employees).

2. **Agent activation model**: Automatic progressive activation (agents start doing things after observation period) or explicit opt-in (boss must click "activate" for each agent role)? Automatic is the PACT-lite vision but may trigger trust issues.

3. **LLM dependency**: Which agent roles require LLM and which are purely deterministic? Current architecture: Payroll is deterministic. Advisory uses LLM. Leave approval is rules-based. Compliance monitoring uses LLM for interpretation. Recruitment uses LLM for screening.

4. **Open source boundary**: Is the PACT domain configuration (templates, envelopes, regulatory mappings) open source, or is it the commercial layer? If open source, the moat is execution speed and community. If proprietary, it conflicts with Foundation independence principles.

5. **Initial go-to-market**: Direct to SME bosses, or through accountants/HR consultants as channel partners? Accountants serve hundreds of SME clients and could drive adoption.
